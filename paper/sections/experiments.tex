\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Models and datasets.} We evaluate on ResNet-19 with
threshold-dependent batch normalisation (tdBN), Spikformer, and SDT-V3
(173M); VGG-16 is excluded from all datasets. The primary dataset is
CIFAR-10, where results have accumulated to the point of statistical
comparison; CIFAR-100 currently has one run per condition, CIFAR10-DVS
requires a rerun with the proposed regulariser, and SDT-V3 is validated on a
separate machine. This section reports the CIFAR-10 / ResNet-19 combination,
the only one with enough repetitions for the comparisons below.

\paragraph{Training.} All arms share $T{=}4$ time steps, 310 epochs, batch
size 100, AdamW with a cosine learning-rate schedule and weight decay
$2\times10^{-2}$, tdBN, label smoothing 0.1, and RandAugment + CutMix +
RandomErasing (CutMix disabled after epoch 200). The surrogate gradient is
\texttt{asym} (bias 0.8 for ResNet, 0.6 for VGG-16/Spikformer); this choice
is inherited from the codebase, not our contribution, and is applied
identically to every arm. One run takes approximately 31 hours (27.2h for
the unregularised baseline).

\paragraph{Comparison arms.} We compare four conditions: no regularisation,
the layer-wise $L_2$ penalty (Plain $L_2$), the $1{-}$softmax weighting
(1-softmax), and the proposed regulariser (Ours).

\paragraph{Seeds.} We do not fix a global random seed. Fixing
\texttt{run\_seed} makes \texttt{tf.data} replay the same augmentation order
every epoch, which collapsed accuracy from 96.65\% to 95.72\% and inflated
the run-to-run standard deviation eightfold in a control experiment; it is
also not reproducible across GPU runs even at a fixed seed. Repeated runs
are therefore repeated re-runs with no seed control, and the per-run tags
(s1, s2, \ldots) are arbitrary labels, not seed identifiers.

\paragraph{Pre-registered selection rules.} Applied symmetrically to the
unregularised baseline, the proposed method, and every comparison arm: (1) a
run is excluded if it does not complete all 310 epochs; (2) a run is
excluded if its spike ratio between epoch 30 and epoch 1 in training-mode
logging, $S_{30}/S_1$, falls below 0.19 (an early-collapse indicator); (3)
if more than 4 valid runs remain after (1)-(2), only the top 4 by
validation accuracy are kept. All comparisons target 5 runs per condition;
the number retained under these rules is reported as $n$ in each table.
Full run logs, including excluded runs and their exclusion reason, are in
the appendix.

\paragraph{Run-set freeze.} The run set for Table~\ref{tab:t1_main} is
frozen as of 2026-09-14. A sixth baseline run completed that day and is
included under rule (3) above, together with the five baseline runs
collected earlier; rule (3) then drops the two lowest-accuracy runs from
the resulting set of six. Runs completing after this date are out of scope
for this table without a separate re-freeze decision.

\paragraph{Spike counting.} Every spike count reported in this section uses
the eval/test protocol. The same model produces a different spike count in
training mode (up to $1.41\times$ higher); training-mode counts are used
only for the early-collapse check in rule (2) above, never in a reported
table.

\paragraph{Reporting.} Tables report the epoch with the best validation
accuracy for each run. Column order is fixed throughout: train loss, train
accuracy, validation loss, validation accuracy, spike count. When a
condition has more than one run, every individual run is listed, with the
group mean directly below.

\subsection{Main result}
\label{sec:exp-main}

Table~\ref{tab:t1_main} compares the unregularised baseline against the
proposed regulariser at three strengths ($\rho \in \{2, 3, 4\}\times
10^{-3}$), Plain $L_2$ at three strengths ($\lambda \in \{1, 2.5,
5\}\times10^{-7}$), and 1-softmax at $\lambda = 1\times10^{-7}$, all on
ResNet-19 / CIFAR-10.

The baseline (regularisation off, $n=4$) reaches $96.720\%$ validation
accuracy at $488{,}619$ spikes. At $\rho=3\times10^{-3}$ ($n=4$), the
proposed regulariser reaches $96.650\%$ at $235{,}802$ spikes --- a
$51.7\%$ reduction in spike count, with the two accuracy figures $0.070$
percentage points apart, about one baseline run-to-run standard deviation
($0.067$). Following the pre-registered no-loss threshold (baseline mean
minus two standard deviations, $96.585\%$ for this combination), $96.650\%$
clears it: the spike reduction at $\rho=3\times10^{-3}$ comes with no
measurable accuracy cost.

The other conditions in Table~\ref{tab:t1_main} show where Plain $L_2$ and
1-softmax land on the same accuracy-spike plane: Plain $L_2$ reaches
$96.550\%$ at $246{,}865$ spikes ($\lambda=2.5\times10^{-7}$, $n=4$) and
$96.517\%$ at $184{,}387$ spikes ($\lambda=5\times10^{-7}$, $n=4$);
1-softmax reaches $96.513\%$ at $194{,}609$ spikes ($n=3$). We report these
figures only to place the proposed regulariser's landing point among the
comparison arms on the spike axis; we do not claim an accuracy ordering
among the four conditions. The gap between the proposed regulariser and
Plain $L_2$ at matched settings is within run-to-run noise for the sample
sizes collected so far, and we do not assert it as an accuracy advantage.

\subsection{Ablation}
\label{sec:exp-ablation}

We ablate three components of the proposed regulariser -- the sub-threshold
term, the single-step application (the spike-count loss applied only at
$t=T$ instead of at every time step), and the $1{-}$ inversion -- removing
each in turn while holding the ratio controller's target $\rho$ fixed at
$4\times10^{-3}$, the same setting used for the proposed regulariser in
Table~\ref{tab:t1_main}. Table~\ref{tab:t3_ablation} (tier A of the design
in spec \S 12-4) reports this same-$\rho$ comparison. Removing a component
changes the active neuron budget that the ratio controller solves against,
so the strength that $\rho=4\times10^{-3}$ resolves to differs per arm and
the landing point in spike count moves accordingly -- that movement is
itself part of the result, not noise to be averaged away. A second,
spike-matched tier of this table (targeting the $\approx$192K landing point
of the proposed regulariser) is designed but not executed: it needs 12
additional runs that have not been approved, and is left as a commented
block in the table source pending that approval.

Removing the $1{-}$ inversion moves the landing point from 192K spikes
(Ours) to 437,011 -- within 10\% of the unregularised baseline's 488,619 --
while $\rho$ is unchanged. We read this as evidence that the inversion is a
precondition for the method to work at all, not a tunable strength: the gap
is 2.3$\times$, large enough that no standard-deviation test is needed to
make the point, and the validation accuracy in that row (96.565\%) looks
ordinary only if the spike column is ignored. Without the inversion,
neurons that fall silent contribute a reward of zero and drop out of the
loss, so the ratio controller sees a much larger neuron budget and
auto-solves for a correspondingly weaker average strength; the residual
gradient that remains lands on neurons already well above threshold, where
the surrogate gradient used here is flat.

Removing the sub-threshold term moves the landing point only modestly (192K
to 148K) but is the one ablation with a confirmed accuracy cost: $-0.222$
percentage points against the fitted accuracy-spike curve, at $3.5\sigma$
and $n=4$ -- the only one of the three components with this statistic
computed at this sample size. Removing the single-step application moves
the landing point to 159K; we do not report a curve offset for this arm or
for the inversion ablation, since neither has been run through the same
regression at a large enough sample to justify a number, and
Table~\ref{tab:t3_ablation} shows \texttt{--} rather than a fabricated
value.

The rightmost column of Table~\ref{tab:t3_ablation} reports spikes per
active neuron from a separate neuron-level extraction, whose run count
differs from the accuracy columns and is cited per row; the sub-threshold
and inversion ablations use $n=3$ and $n=2$ respectively, both below 4, so
we report the count rather than inventing a standard deviation. Both
ablations raise spikes-per-neuron above Ours' $1.4790$ ($n=11$) --
$1.4860$ without the sub-threshold term and $1.5300$ without the inversion
-- consistent with the broader ordering in which removing regularisation
strength raises the firing intensity per neuron. Taken together, the three
columns point to two different effects: the sub-threshold term's
contribution is on the accuracy axis, and the inversion's contribution is
on the landing-point (concentration) axis. They are not the same effect,
and neither result depends on claiming an accuracy advantage over any
comparison arm.

\subsection{Training cost}
\label{sec:exp-cost}

Table~\ref{tab:t4_cost} reports wall-clock training cost, averaged over 310
epochs, with a per-run standard deviation of 25--30\,ms ($\approx$4\%).
Turning on any spike-count regulariser costs 7--33\% over the unregularised
baseline; the proposed regulariser costs 10\%, less than every other
regularised comparison arm but not less than the unregularised baseline
itself. The correct statement is that turning on regularisation costs 10\%,
which is less than any alternative tested here -- not that the proposed
regulariser is free, and not only that it is 2.4\% cheaper than Plain
$L_2$ (695 vs.\ 712 ms/step), which by itself would understate the cost
relative to the unregularised baseline. Applying the same loss at every
time step instead of once at $t=T$ (the last row of
Table~\ref{tab:t4_cost}) costs 33\%, isolating the single-step
application's contribution to keeping the proposed regulariser's overhead
low.

\subsection{Generality}
\label{sec:exp-generality}

CIFAR-100 currently has one run per condition for the regularised arms
(baseline has $n=3$). The unregularised baseline reaches $80.720\%$
validation accuracy at 446,134 spikes; Plain $L_2$ at
$\lambda=5\times10^{-7}$ ($n=1$) reaches $80.66\%$ at 253,049 spikes; the
proposed regulariser at $\rho=3\times10^{-3}$ ($n=1$) reaches $80.37\%$ at
211,350 spikes, a 52.6\% reduction from the baseline. These numbers, in
Table~\ref{tab:t8_generality} in the appendix, are the only generality
measurement available: with $n=1$ for the two regularised arms there is no
error bar, and because their landing points do not match, no
accuracy-spike curve can be fit for this combination the way it can for
CIFAR-10 (Table~\ref{tab:t1_main}). Spikformer and CIFAR10-DVS have only
been swept with an earlier, superseded weighting scheme and have not been
re-run with the proposed regulariser; SDT-V3 (173M parameters) has the
regulariser ported to its codebase but training has not started, on a
separate machine. We report the CIFAR-100 numbers as the only measurement
that exists, not as evidence that the CIFAR-10 result transfers to another
dataset or architecture -- that would require $n\geq3$ per condition on
CIFAR-100 and at least one completed re-run on a second architecture or
dataset, neither of which is available yet.
