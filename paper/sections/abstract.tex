Spiking neural networks are valued for sparse, event-driven computation, but
published accuracy numbers say little about how many spikes a network
actually needed to reach them. Trained the usual way -- ResNet-19, CIFAR-10, $T{=}4$, no penalty on
firing -- the network settles into 488,619 spikes; adding a per-neuron
spike-count regulariser during training removes 51.7\% of them with no
measurable accuracy cost. This reduction alone does not distinguish the
proposed regulariser from a plain per-layer $L_2$ penalty on spike count
(Plain $L_2$), which lands on the same accuracy-spike curve. The two differ
underneath that curve: at a matched spike budget, the proposed regulariser
activates 13.1\% fewer neurons than Plain $L_2$ (regression coefficient
0.869, $t=-21.5$, $n=21$), concentrating firing onto fewer neurons rather
than weakening every neuron's firing uniformly. Our contribution is this
neuron-usage measurement and the strength control that produces it, set
through a single ratio against the task loss rather than a hand-tuned
coefficient or a pre-set spike target. Validation to date is limited to
ResNet-19 on CIFAR-10 in simulation; we do not claim an accuracy advantage
over Plain $L_2$, generality beyond this architecture and dataset, or a
measured benefit on neuromorphic hardware.
