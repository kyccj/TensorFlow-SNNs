Spiking neural networks are valued for sparse, event-driven computation, but
published accuracy numbers say little about how many spikes a network
needed to reach them. Trained the usual way -- ResNet-19, CIFAR-10, $T{=}4$, no penalty on
firing -- the network settles into 488,619 spikes; adding a per-neuron
spike-count regulariser during training removes 51.7\% of them with no
measurable accuracy cost. This reduction alone does not distinguish the
proposed regulariser from a plain per-layer $L_2$ penalty on spike count
(Plain $L_2$), which lands on the same accuracy-spike curve. The two differ
underneath that curve: at a matched spike budget, the proposed regulariser
activates 13.1\% fewer neurons than Plain $L_2$, concentrating firing onto fewer neurons
(in early and middle layers) rather than weakening every neuron's firing uniformly. Our contributions are this
neuron-usage measurement and a strength control that sets the
regulariser through a single ratio against the task loss, rather than a
hand-tuned coefficient or target -- a tentative distinction from prior approaches. Validation to date is limited to
ResNet-19 on CIFAR-10 in simulation, without an accuracy advantage over
Plain $L_2$, generality beyond this setting, or a measured
neuromorphic-hardware benefit.
